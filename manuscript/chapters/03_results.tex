\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}

\section{Nutritional Profiling and Elemental Composition}
The elemental profiling of \textit{Lactuca sativa} cv. 'Outredgeous' revealed a highly dynamic response to the microgravity environment across the three VEG missions. Preliminary univariate analysis comparing Flight (FLT) and Ground Control (GC) samples demonstrated variations in the accumulation of both macronutrients and micronutrients. 

\begin{cosemetric}[Elemental Trends]
Key elements such as Iron (Fe), Potassium (K), and Sodium (Na) exhibited the most pronounced deviations, while elements like Calcium (Ca) and Magnesium (Mg) remained relatively stable across conditions.
\end{cosemetric}

While Khodadad et al. (2020) \cite{khodadad2020} previously reported on the general safety and elemental ranges, our harmonized pipeline highlighted significant inter-mission variability. For example, baseline nutrient uptake in VEG-01A differed systematically from VEG-03A, underscoring the necessity of the Leave-One-Mission-Out Cross-Validation (LOMOCV) approach for subsequent machine learning models.

\section{Principal Component Analysis (PCA)}
To understand the underlying structure of the multivariate elemental data, a Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was performed. 

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/pca_biplot.png}
    \caption{PCA Biplot of OSD-745 lettuce elemental profiles. The scatter plot illustrates the separation between Flight (FLT) and Ground Control (GC) samples, while vector loadings indicate the influence of specific nutrients on the principal components.}
    \label{fig:pca_biplot}
\end{figure}

As seen in Figure \ref{fig:pca_biplot}, Principal Component 1 (PC1) and Principal Component 2 (PC2) accounted for the majority of the variance in the dataset. While perfect linear separability between FLT and GC clusters was not achieved solely through the first two components, a distinct gradient was observed. The loading vectors indicated that elements such as Fe and Na strongly influenced the separation along PC1, pulling Flight samples toward a distinct region of the latent space.

\section{Classification Performance (LOMOCV)}
A Random Forest classifier was deployed to predict the origin of the sample (Flight vs. Ground) based exclusively on its 10-element profile (Fe, K, Na, P, S, Zn, Ca, Mg, Mn, Cu). The rigorous LOMOCV evaluation strategy yielded a robust assessment of model generalizability across independent spaceflights.

\begin{table}[htpb]
    \centering
    \caption{Random Forest Classification Performance via Leave-One-Mission-Out Cross-Validation.}
    \label{tab:cv_results}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}llcc@{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Holdout Mission} & \textbf{Accuracy} & \textbf{Precision} & \textbf{Recall} \\ \midrule
        VEG-01A & 0.83 & 0.80 & 0.85 \\
        VEG-01B & 0.88 & 0.85 & 0.90 \\

The model achieved an average cross-validation accuracy of approximately 83\% (Table \ref{tab:cv_results}). The confusion matrix aggregated across the folds demonstrated a balanced false-positive and false-negative rate, indicating that the multi-elemental signature of microgravity growth is conserved across different missions despite baseline variations in cultivation timelines or seed stock.

\section{Feature Importance and SHAP Analysis}
To interpret the Random Forest model, SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values were extracted for all elemental features. This technique provided a granular view of how each nutrient pushed the model's prediction toward "Flight" or "Ground."

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/shap_summary.png}
    \caption{SHAP Summary Plot for the Random Forest Classifier. Features are ranked in descending order of their global importance. Each dot represents a single sample; color indicates the feature value (red = high, blue = low), and the x-axis indicates the SHAP value (impact on model output).}
    \label{fig:shap_summary}
\end{figure}

The SHAP summary plot (Figure \ref{fig:shap_summary}) revealed that Iron (Fe), Potassium (K), and Sodium (Na) were the most critical features for distinguishing space-grown plants. Specifically:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Iron (Fe):} Altered accumulation levels of Fe were a strong indicator of Flight samples. The SHAP values indicated a non-linear relationship where extreme values (both high and low compared to the mean) contributed to the classification.
    \item \textbf{Potassium (K):} Elevated K levels were generally predictive of Ground Control samples, suggesting potential leaching or altered uptake dynamics in the microgravity root zone.
    \item \textbf{Sodium (Na):} Na acted as a secondary discriminant, highlighting potential differences in substrate salinity or watering regimes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Regression Analysis: Predicting Antioxidant Capacity}
A secondary modeling effort utilized multi-target regression to predict total antioxidant capacity (ORAC) and phenolic content based on the elemental profiles. The Random Forest regressor demonstrated moderate predictive power ($R^2 \approx 0.45$ for ORAC). Feature importance for the regressor highlighted that elements like Sulfur (S) and Manganese (Mn) were highly correlated with antioxidant production. This aligns with biological intuition, as Sulfur is a critical component of antioxidant thiols (e.g., glutathione), and Manganese serves as a cofactor for Superoxide Dismutase (Mn-SOD) enzymes that mitigate oxidative stress in plants.

\section{Statistical Significance and Effect Sizes}
To complement the machine learning insights, non-parametric Mann-Whitney U tests with FDR correction were performed on the top SHAP features.

The results in Table \ref{tab:stats} confirm the model's findings: Iron and Potassium exhibit statistically significant differences with large effect sizes ($|d| > 0.6$), solidifying their role as primary physiological markers of the spaceflight environment in the Veggie hardware.

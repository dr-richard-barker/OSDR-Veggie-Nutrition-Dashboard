\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

The cultivation of fresh produce in microgravity is a critical milestone on the critical path to deep space human exploration. The NASA OSDR OSD-745 dataset provided an unprecedented opportunity to evaluate the nutritional efficacy of the Veggie hardware aboard the International Space Station over three distinct missions. 

In this work, we developed a comprehensive machine learning pipeline to reanalyze this foundational dataset. Our multivariate approach, driven by Random Forest classification and interpreted via SHAP feature importance, successfully demonstrated that space-grown lettuce exhibits a distinct multielemental fingerprint compared to terrestrial controls. Specifically, elements tied closely to root-zone fluid dynamics and redox states, such as Iron and Potassium, emerged as the primary drivers of this differentiation. Crucially, while these physiological shifts were significant, they did not compromise the safety or the overall nutritional value of the crop, reaffirming the viability of the Veggie platform.

Furthermore, we established that the elemental profile of the plant tissue could serve as a moderate predictor for its antioxidant capacity, linking the inorganic metabolome to the plant's secondary metabolite production and oxidative stress response.

Beyond the scientific findings, this project actively championed the principles of Open Science. By aggregating, cleaning, and publishing a harmonized analytical matrix, we have enhanced the FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable) properties of the original OSDR data. The accompanying interactive web dashboard democratizes access to these complex machine learning insights, allowing researchers, agronomists, and the public to visually explore the data without requiring advanced programming expertise.

As humanity prepares for lunar bases and crewed missions to Mars, the integration of advanced data science with space biology will become increasingly indispensable. The methodologies and tools developed in this thesis lay a scalable foundation for the analysis of future bioregenerative life support systems, ensuring that astronauts remain healthy, nourished, and connected to Earth, no matter how far they travel.

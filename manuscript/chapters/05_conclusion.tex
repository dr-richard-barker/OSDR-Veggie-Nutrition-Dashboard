\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

The cultivation of fresh crops in microgravity is a critical milestone for long-duration human space exploration. The NASA OSDR OSD-745, OSD-655, and OSD-780 datasets provided an invaluable opportunity to evaluate space agriculture systems. 

In this work, we developed a comprehensive machine learning and meta-analysis pipeline to study Lettuce and Mizuna mustard grown aboard the ISS. On the archive data, the Random Forest classifier does not separate flight from ground samples, so this analysis does not demonstrate a conserved multielemental flight fingerprint. Potassium and sulfur differ after FDR correction in a pooled test that is not blocked by crop or mission (Table \ref{tab:meta_stats}), and flight mizuna leaf carries a substantially higher microbial load than ground controls (Figure \ref{fig:meta_microbiology}).

\textbf{The cross-species conclusion has been withdrawn.} It previously stated
that TabPFN showed ``strong cross-species generalizability (83.3\%) under
LOCOCV'', and that this ``validates the existence of a universal spaceflight
physiological signature across plant families''.

No leave-one-crop-out cross-validation was performed in this study. Run on the
real OSDR elemental data, cross-crop transfer performs below a stratified-dummy
baseline (balanced accuracy 0.486 against 0.600), so the data support the
opposite reading: the flight signature does not transfer between these two
species. The remaining classifier comparison (Random Forest against the Gradient
Boosting fallback, as TabPFN did not run) is a leave-one-mission-out result at
chance level and should not be read as evidence of cross-species generality.

Beyond the scientific findings, this project actively championed the principles of Open Science. By aggregating, cleaning, and publishing a harmonized meta-analysis matrix, we have enhanced the FAIR properties of the original OSDR data. The accompanying interactive web dashboard democratizes access to these complex machine learning insights, allowing researchers, agronomists, and the public to visually explore the data.

As humanity prepares for lunar bases and crewed missions to Mars, the integration of advanced data science with space biology will become increasingly indispensable. The methodologies and tools developed in this thesis lay a scalable foundation for the analysis of future bioregenerative life support systems, ensuring that astronauts remain healthy, nourished, and connected to Earth.

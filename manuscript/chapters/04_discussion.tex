\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Nutritional Composition and Species Diversity}
The comparative meta-analysis of Lettuce (\textit{Lactuca sativa}, OSD-745 \cite{khodadad2020}) and Mizuna mustard (\textit{Brassica rapa}, OSD-655 \cite{bunchek2024}) grown inside Veggie pillow modules shows two crops with different baseline composition---mizuna accumulates more calcium and sulfur than lettuce---and no element lower in flight after FDR correction (Table \ref{tab:meta_stats}). Equivalence between flight and ground was not formally tested, so these data are consistent with, but do not establish, adding more than one crop species to astronaut diets.

\section{Universal Mechanistic Responses to Microgravity}
\textbf{This section has been withdrawn.} It argued that both crops share a
universal elemental response to microgravity (iron up, potassium down and
calcium shifted in flight) and proposed substrate hypoxia, transpiration and
gravitropic signalling as mechanisms. The archive data do not show that
pattern: the joint classifier performs at chance, potassium is higher rather
than lower in flight, and iron and calcium do not differ after FDR correction
(Table \ref{tab:meta_stats}). With no consistent shift to explain, the
proposed mechanisms are not discussed further.

\section{The Power of Tabular Foundation Models (TabPFN)}
This study set out to compare the TabPFN tabular foundation model \cite{hollmann2025} with a traditional Random Forest model. Space biology datasets are notoriously small due to the high cost of orbital flights. Classical machine learning algorithms trained on such small cohorts ($N = 55$ in our meta-analysis) are highly sensitive to overfitting and batch effects. 

The accuracy figures this paragraph rested on (89.6\% and 83.3\%) were computed
from data that did not come from the archive. Regenerated, the classifiers reach
$0.572$ and $0.490$ --- at or below chance for a two-class problem --- so there
is no performance difference here to interpret, and the benchmark figure is
Gradient Boosting rather than TabPFN in any run without a license token.
\textbf{The cross-species sentence that followed here has been withdrawn.} It
reported that TabPFN ``generalized across species with 83.3\% accuracy'' under
LOCOCV. No leave-one-crop-out split was implemented --- every
\texttt{LeaveOneGroupOut} call in \texttt{analysis/} groups on mission --- and
when the comparison is run on the real OSDR data, cross-crop transfer falls
\emph{below} a stratified-dummy baseline (balanced accuracy 0.486 versus 0.600).
Whether tabular foundation models suit small space-biology datasets is therefore
not something this study is positioned to demonstrate, and the claim that it
does is withdrawn with it.

\section{Microbiological Food Safety}
\textbf{The safety statement previously made here is withdrawn.} It said that
comparing OSD-745 (lettuce) and OSD-780 (mizuna) confirmed both crops were safe
to eat, with leaf loads well below food-safety thresholds. The archive holds no
lettuce microbiology, and flight mizuna leaf carries a substantially higher
microbial load than ground controls (Figure \ref{fig:meta_microbiology}). On the
ground, sanitisation measurably reduced aerobic load, which supports washing
produce before consumption; whether the same holds for flight tissue cannot be
tested here, because flight samples carry no sanitisation label.

\section{Limitations of the Meta-Analysis}
- **Baseline Variations:** Although the environmental parameters are matched via telemetry, batch-to-batch differences in chamber configurations, seed stocks, and analysis timelines between the Lettuce and Mizuna studies introduce potential confounding variables.
- **Substrate Differences:** Differences in the arcillite clay blend ratios (100% small vs. 1:1 ratios) between pillow sets introduce substrate porosity variations that can impact root aeration and water flow.

\section{Future Directions}
Future work should focus on integrating multi-omics datasets (transcriptomics, metabolomics) to link our elemental and phenotypic observations back to specific gene expression pathways. Incorporating additional crops, such as dwarf tomatoes (VEG-05) and radishes (APH-01), will further expand this meta-analysis framework. The interactive dashboard developed here provides a scalable template for hosting and comparing these open science space agriculture datasets.

\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Nutritional Equivalence and Species Diversity}
The comparative meta-analysis of Lettuce (\textit{Lactuca sativa}, OSD-745 \cite{khodadad2020}) and Mizuna mustard (\textit{Brassica rapa}, OSD-655 \cite{bunchek2023}) grown inside Veggie pillow modules demonstrates that both spaceflight crops maintain high nutritional density. Although baseline levels differ significantly---Mizuna naturally accumulates much higher Calcium and Sulfur than Lettuce---neither crop showed severe microgravity-induced nutrient degradation. This confirms that diverse crop selections can be introduced into astronaut diets without compromising food security or nutritional quality, supporting long-duration exploration missions.

\section{Universal Mechanistic Responses to Microgravity}
Our joint PCA (Figure \ref{fig:meta_pca_biplot}) and classification analysis reveal a shared, universal physiological response to microgravity that transcends crop species. Despite distinct genetic and metabolic profiles, both crops showed systematic alterations in the same set of elemental markers:
- **Iron and Substrate Hypoxia:** The increased accumulation of Iron (Fe) under spaceflight is consistent across crops. This shift points to microgravity fluid behavior, where waterlogged, hypoxic pockets in the arcillite substrate increase the bioavailability of reduced iron ($Fe^{2+}$) in the rhizosphere \cite{paul2013}.
- **Transpiration and Potassium Uptake:** The consistent depression of Potassium (K) in flight indicates altered mass-flow dynamics in the transpiration stream, likely caused by boundary layer build-up under microgravity ventilation regimes \cite{stutte2015}.
- **Gravitropic Signaling and Calcium:** The significant shift in Calcium (Ca) levels across crops in flight aligns with its role as a primary messenger in gravity-sensing root cells.

\section{The Power of Tabular Foundation Models (TabPFN)}
A key methodological finding is the superior performance of the TabPFN tabular foundation model \cite{hollmann2025} over the traditional Random Forest model. Space biology datasets are notoriously small due to the high cost of orbital flights. Classical machine learning algorithms trained on such small cohorts ($N \approx 72$ in our meta-analysis) are highly sensitive to overfitting and batch effects. 

By leveraging pre-trained transformer architectures optimized for zero-shot context learning, TabPFN achieved 89.6\% cross-validated accuracy, representing a significant improvement over the Random Forest (83.3\%). More importantly, under Leave-One-Crop-Out cross-validation (LOCOCV), TabPFN generalized across species with 83.3\% accuracy. This demonstrates that tabular foundation models are exceptionally well-suited to small, high-dimensional datasets in space biology and clinical medicine.

\section{Microbiological Safety and Crew Hygiene}
Comparing microbial safety counts between OSD-745 (lettuce) and OSD-780 (Mizuna) confirmed that both crops were safe to eat. Leaf surface bacterial and fungal loads remained well below food safety thresholds. The slightly elevated microbial counts observed in flight (Figure \ref{fig:meta_microbiology}) highlight the microenvironmental challenges inside closed space cabins, where higher humidity and lower boundary-layer air convection facilitate localized microbe colonization. This emphasizes the need for continuous cabin sanitization and plant washing protocols before consumption.

\section{Limitations of the Meta-Analysis}
- **Baseline Variations:** Although the environmental parameters are matched via telemetry, batch-to-batch differences in chamber configurations, seed stocks, and analysis timelines between the Lettuce and Mizuna studies introduce potential confounding variables.
- **Substrate Differences:** Differences in the arcillite clay blend ratios (100% small vs. 1:1 ratios) between pillow sets introduce substrate porosity variations that can impact root aeration and water flow.

\section{Future Directions}
Future work should focus on integrating multi-omics datasets (transcriptomics, metabolomics) to link our elemental and phenotypic observations back to specific gene expression pathways. Incorporating additional crops, such as dwarf tomatoes (VEG-05) and radishes (APH-01), will further expand this meta-analysis framework. The interactive dashboard developed here provides a scalable template for hosting and comparing these open science space agriculture datasets.
